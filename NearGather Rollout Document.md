# **The biggest skeptic objections**

These are the things that could kill the business.

### **Forced RSVP contribution could annoy guests**

This is the \#1 product risk.

I **would still force the contribution**, because that's your differentiator.

But require:

**video OR audio OR text**

not video.

And give organizers an:

**Exempt this guest**

control for elderly guests, accessibility needs or unusual situations.

The KPI to obsess over is:

> **RSVP abandonment caused by the required contribution.**

If it is materially high, we adjust the interaction—not abandon the principle.

---

### **Free RSVP competition is brutal**

Zola currently offers wedding websites and RSVP/meal-preference collection for free. [zola.com](https://www.zola.com/wedding-planning/website)

Therefore:

> “Better RSVP management”

will **never** be enough.

NearGather sells:

> **Everyone who says yes leaves you part of your story.**

Different purchase category.

---

### **Physical fulfillment can wreck a digital business**

Deluxe creates:

printing errors  
shipping issues  
event deadlines  
lost packages  
custom-design labor

Do **not** hand-design every package.

Templates \+ automated personalization \+ print fulfillment.

And enforce:

> Deluxe must be ordered X days before event.

Otherwise one frantic bride will destroy your support margins.

---

### **The contest can cheapen the emotional experience**

Don't call it:

**Leaderboard**

or:

**Top User**

Call it:

### **Memory Maker**

Don't show public points unless the organizer chooses to.

Reveal the winner after the event.

The gamification should be almost invisible.

---

### **MMS video is messy**

High-quality video should ultimately upload over the web rather than depend entirely on carrier MMS constraints.

The experience can still begin:

> “Text us a video.”

NearGather can ingest it when feasible or return a one-tap secure full-quality upload link when necessary.

Don't architect your preservation product around carrier-compressed video.
